Where we fish

Two rivers worth knowing by heart.

Richard guides year-round on Northern California water — from the cold tailwater below Lake Berryessa to the gold-country riffles of the lower Yuba. Every trip is chosen for the conditions of the day, not a fixed itinerary.

See all trips
Richard's home water

Putah Creek

A 4.5-mile tailwater running clean and cold out from beneath Lake Berryessa, inside the Snow Mountain & Berryessa National Monument. Wild rainbow trout hold in water that rewards a good drift and a quiet approach — and Richard has logged thousands of hours learning exactly where.

Yuba River

Trout · Steelhead

The lower fork at Marysville, in old gold country. Bigger water, longer casts, and a genuinely good chance at a steelhead on the swing.

American River

Shad · April–May

A short, brilliant season. When the shad run, the American turns into the most forgiving place in the region to learn a two-handed cast.

Your guide

Richard Loft

Richard has been fishing since he was ten years old, hitchhiking to Silverwood Lake before he was old enough to drive. A friend at Sweeney's Sports in Napa handed him a fly rod on Putah Creek nine years later — in an old pair of neoprene waders — and that was that. He has been on the water nearly every week since.

He is a licensed guide, a conservationist, and a patient teacher. If you run into him on the creek you will usually find Rusty, his rescue dog, close behind — and Richard either shouting "WHOO-HOO!" on a hook-up or picking up trash someone else left behind.

I would do just about anything to get a chance to fish; to stand out in a river and let my thoughts wander. It's the most peaceful place in the world to me. Richard Loft, founder

Stewardship

A guide who gives back to the creek.

Guiding is only part of the job. Richard has partnered with Putah Creek Trout and the Department of Fish & Wildlife's Heritage Wild Trout Program since 2008, taking part in annual California Coastal Clean-Ups, creek surveys and habitat restoration.

He has also mentored young anglers through the Napa RCD's SLEWS youth program, and studied wildlife and fisheries biology at Napa Junior College — because, as he puts it, a river you understand is a river you can protect.

What we practise on the water

  • Catch-and-release only, with fish kept wet and in the net
  • Barbless-friendly flies and careful handling in warm water
  • Packing out everything we carry in — plus a little more
